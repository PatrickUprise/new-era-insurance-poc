Common Questions

What is high-value home insurance in Miami?

High-value home insurance is coverage designed for properties whose replacement cost or features go beyond a standard homeowners policy. In Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach, this often includes higher dwelling limits, agreed-value options, and attention to wind, flood, and valuable personal property.

What does a condo association master policy cover in Florida?

Under Florida Statute 718.111, a residential condominium association must carry adequate property insurance based on replacement cost. The master policy typically covers the building as originally installed and common elements, while excluding certain interior items that become the unit owner’s responsibility under an HO-6 policy.

How often does a Florida condo association need a replacement cost appraisal?

Florida law requires the replacement cost of the insured property to be determined at least once every three years through an independent insurance appraisal or an update of a prior appraisal.

What is the difference between the association master policy and an HO-6 policy?

The master policy covers the association’s property and common elements. An HO-6 unit-owner policy covers personal belongings, interior items the statute assigns to the owner (such as floor coverings, appliances, and built-in cabinets), personal liability, loss of use, and loss assessment coverage.

How do wind deductibles work on high-value homes and condo associations in South Florida?

Wind deductibles are often expressed as a percentage of the insured value. For high-value homes and condo master policies, the board or homeowner sets the percentage based on available funds and risk tolerance. The resulting deductible is typically treated as a common expense for association-insured property.
